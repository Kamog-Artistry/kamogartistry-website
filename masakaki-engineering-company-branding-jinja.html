Challenge

An Engineering Company Brand That Needed to Work Across Many Business Touchpoints

A brand system for an engineering business has to do more than look attractive on screen. It must reproduce properly on documents, business cards, stamps, signage, banners and safety wear. In this case, the starting point was a low-resolution existing mark that needed to be redrawn and strengthened before the rest of the rollout could happen.

The challenge was not only visual consistency. It was also production readiness. Every item needed to feel related, usable and realistic for how the company works in actual office and field environments. That meant the identity had to carry through both formal presentation materials and practical everyday items without losing clarity.

Logo Audit and Redraw

The process began with cleaning up the existing low-resolution logo so it could scale properly and support future production.

Stationery and Identity Rollout

The new mark was then extended into letterheads, business cards, official stamp applications and framed presentation items.

Office and Display Branding

Door labels, office signage and a pull up banner were added to strengthen visibility inside and around the workspace.

Operational Workwear Branding

Reflector branding and QR code integration helped push the identity into practical day-to-day field use.

Strong branding for a technical company is not just about the logo itself. It is about how that logo behaves across identity design, official stationery, signage, display materials and branded workwear without losing consistency.
Logo Redraw

Cleaning Up the Original Mark So It Could Support a Better Brand Rollout

The first major step was the logo redraw. For many growing companies, an old logo may already hold recognition, but it can still create production limitations when it is low resolution, uneven or difficult to reproduce. By redrawing the Masakaki Engineering mark in a cleaner format, the identity became more reliable for logo usage across multiple outputs.

This stage also helped align the later corporate identity items. A cleaner logo gives better results on letterheads and branded stationery, sharper print performance on business cards, and more controlled reproduction on branded garments and safety wear.

Stationery & Print

Building Business Identity Through Letterheads, Premium Cards and Official Stamp Assets

After the redraw, the project moved into branded stationery and print. This included letterhead design, business card layout, premium non-tearable card production and official stamp preparation. These are the kinds of materials that shape how a company is perceived in meetings, document exchange and everyday professional interaction.

For an engineering company, strong stationery matters because the business often operates through quotations, technical correspondence, formal letters and direct introductions. Well-made stationery supports credibility. In this case, the mix of branded stationery design, premium business card printing and official stamp handling created a stronger working identity.

Reflector Branding

Applying the Brand to Safety Wear Through DTF Production and QR Code Integration

One of the strongest parts of this case study is the move into branded reflector wear. For a technical company, safety wear is not just merchandise. It is part of the real operational environment. Branding these items well helps the company look more coordinated on-site, more recognisable in the field and more professional when teams are working around clients or partners.

The reflector workflow included DTF transfer layout, heat press setup, application to the garments, front view presentation, back view QR integration and live scan demonstration. That combination makes the work stronger from an SEO and portfolio point of view because it proves Kamog Artistry handles both custom merchandise branding and more technical branded production tied to practical use.
