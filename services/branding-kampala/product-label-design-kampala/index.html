Product Labels Designed for Real Sizes, Trimming & Shelf Clarity

A good label is not “just design.” It’s a production layout that must wrap correctly, remain readable on small containers, and print cleanly after trimming and lamination. In Kampala, label problems often come from wrong sizes, missing bleed, tiny text, or crowded panels. We design labels to survive the real process—so your vendor can print without guesswork.

Our Product Label Design Process

We start with the container and real constraints, then design for shelf clarity, then prepare print-ready exports—so you avoid wasteful reprints.

1) Size + Container Review

You share container dimensions (or a sample). We confirm label size, wrap seam, and any curvature constraints.

2) Content & Hierarchy

We structure the label so customers quickly understand what the product is, the variant, and key benefits—without clutter.

3) Design + Refinement

We design and refine typography, spacing and readability at real size, ensuring the label remains premium and legible.

4) Print Exports

We deliver press-ready files with bleed/safe margins and previews for approval and vendor production.

Technical Specs We Design For

Labels fail when bleed is missing, text is too small, barcodes are squeezed, or the wrap seam cuts important elements. We design for production so printing and trimming in Kampala is smooth.

Real-Size Readability

We test typography at the true label size to ensure ingredients, usage and key info remain readable.

Bleed + Safe Margins

We include bleed and safe zones so trimming doesn’t cut text and edges remain clean after lamination.

Wrap Seam Planning

We keep critical content away from the seam and plan panel breaks for jars and curved bottles.

Barcode/QR Space

We plan clean space for barcode/QR and ensure contrast and quiet zones for scanning.

Common Label Mistakes We Fix in Kampala

Labels can look good on screen and fail in production. Here are common issues we avoid so your first print run comes out right.

Missing Bleed

Edges show white lines after trimming because bleed wasn’t included.

Tiny Text

Ingredients/usage too small—unreadable at real size, especially after lamination.

Bad Wrap Seam

Important content sits on the seam or curve and becomes distorted on bottles/jars.

Barcode Issues

No quiet zone/contrast—barcodes fail to scan in retail settings.
